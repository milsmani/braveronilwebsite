## 1. What DIPG and DMG are (all three sources make this distinction)

- **DIPG = Diffuse Intrinsic Pontine Glioma** — a fast-growing, hard-to-treat tumor
  that forms in the **pons**, a critical part of the **brainstem**. The brainstem
  controls essential functions like **breathing and heartbeat**, which is what makes
  these tumors so dangerous and difficult to treat. *(CT, MPF)*
- **DMG = Diffuse Midline Glioma** — a broader diagnosis for tumors that grow in the
  **"midline" of the brain**: the **thalamus, brainstem (pons), cerebellum, and spinal
  cord**. **Most DIPGs that are biopsied (70–90%) fall under the DMG category**, but
  **not all DMGs are DIPGs** — some appear outside the pons. *(CT, YTF)*
- **Gliomas** arise from **glial cells** — the cells that protect and support neurons.
  **"Diffuse"** means the tumor is **not well-contained**: it grows out into healthy
  tissue so cancer cells mix with normal cells, which is what makes it so hard to
  treat. *(YTF)*
- A specific mutation, **H3 K27M**, is one of the **strongest indicators of a more
  difficult prognosis**; DMGs are often defined by it (WHO: "DMG, H3 K27-altered").
  *(CT, YTF)*
- Both are **WHO grade IV** (the most aggressive grade) and **almost always fatal**.
  *(MPF, YTF)*

## 2. Who it affects

- **Primarily children**, at the heart of childhood. Onset is in **middle childhood**.
- Typical age range varies by source: **ages 4–11** (CT), **peak ages 5–9** (YTF),
  **median age ~7** (MPF). Can rarely occur in younger children and adults. *(all)*
- **~10–15% of all childhood brain tumors.** *(CT)*
- Annual U.S. diagnoses (sources differ — see §9): **~300** (CT) / **400** (MPF) /
  **200–400** (YTF). **~10,000 children worldwide per year.** *(MPF)*

## 3. Effects & symptoms (rapid onset — days to weeks)

- **DIPG leaves kids mentally intact but steals essential bodily functions** —
  depriving a child of the ability to **walk, stand, eat, drink, and even talk**. Their
  **intelligence and awareness often remain intact** even as they lose abilities. *(CT)*
- **Early symptoms:** double vision, reduced eye movement, **facial weakness or
  asymmetry**, arm and leg weakness. *(CT, YTF)*
- Also common: **nausea and vomiting, headaches**, and problems with **walking,
  coordination, vision, speech, and swallowing**. *(YTF)*
- **As the tumor progresses**, it interferes with **breathing and heartbeat, which is
  ultimately fatal.** The final months are excruciatingly difficult. *(CT, YTF)*
- Symptoms **worsen quickly** — families are forced to watch their child lose abilities
  they once had. *(CT)*

## 4. Causes

- **Not hereditary** — not passed from parent to child. *(CT)*
- **Not known to be environmental** — there is **nothing a parent did or did not do**
  that caused it. *(CT, YTF)*
- Believed to be a **developmentally based tumor** — influenced by particular cells
  present in high concentrations while the brain is developing, which fits why it
  most often occurs during **middle childhood**. *(CT)*
- For most patients the **cause is unknown**; the disease structure is complex and
  more research is needed. *(CT, YTF)*

## 5. Diagnosis

- Detected almost exclusively by **MRI** — because of the tight **blood–brain barrier**,
  **cancer markers do not show up in the blood**. *(YTF)*
- The tumor is often **distinctive enough on MRI** (taking up the majority of the pons)
  and presents with such distinctive symptoms (rapid eye in-turning, one-sided
  weakness, loss of balance) that diagnosis can be made **by imaging alone**. *(MPF)*
- **Biopsies** were long avoided (small tissue yield, serious risks, didn't change the
  standard of care) but are **increasingly safe and more common** — recently giving
  researchers the ability to **analyze tumor tissue** for the first time. Some major
  hospitals still don't perform them. *(MPF, CT)*
- Because children usually only get an MRI once there's serious neurologic concern,
  the cancer has often **already begun to spread** by the time it's found. *(YTF)*

## 6. Treatment

- **Radiation therapy is the standard of care / first-line treatment**, given as
  **~30 fractions over a ~6-week period** (whole-brain or proton). *(MPF, YTF)*
- Radiation **extends median survival from a few months to ~11 months**, and
  **75–85% of patients show some symptom improvement** — but **DMGs almost always
  regrow** (recurrence/progression). It is **not a cure**. *(MPF, YTF)*
- **Chemotherapy** has shown **little to no benefit** — hundreds of regimens, including
  bone-marrow-transplant-level doses, have failed to cure DIPG. Some believe certain
  conventional chemos modestly help, but not for every patient and never curative.
  *(MPF, YTF)*
- **Surgery is not possible** — the tumor grows diffusely within the pons, mixing with
  healthy tissue near centers that control vital functions. Removing it is fatal. *(all)*
- **The research frontier:** large national/international working groups are driving the
  next phases of clinical trials — **CED (convection-enhanced delivery: injecting
  treatment directly into the pons), immunotherapies, and new targeted drugs.**
  **ONC201** is a notable recent development (CT highlights its FDA progress). *(MPF, CT)*
- **Treatment protocols have remained essentially unchanged for ~60 years.** *(YTF, MPF)*

## 7. Prognosis

- **DIPG is the #1 deadliest / most lethal pediatric brain tumor**, causing **nearly
  half of all pediatric brain-tumor deaths.** *(MPF, CT)*
- **Median overall survival: ~8–11 months** from diagnosis (MPF cites 9 months; YTF
  cites 8–11; with radiation ~11). *(MPF, YTF)*
- **Less than 10%** survive beyond **2 years**; **less than 1%** survive beyond
  **5 years**. *(YTF)*
- **60+ years with the same prognosis and effectively no survivors.** *(MPF)*
- **Universally fatal** — there is **no cure**. *(YTF, MPF)*

## 8. The case for hope (foundation framing)

- **"For decades, little was understood about DIPG/DMG"** — but **safer brainstem
  biopsies now let researchers analyze tumor tissue**, a genuine turning point. *(CT)*
- New modalities (**CED, immunotherapy, targeted drugs like ONC201**) and collaborative
  trial networks are the first real momentum in a generation. *(MPF)*
- **"Progress is possible, and it is happening because of people who choose to give."**
  Every gift helps **accelerate discoveries, fund clinical trials, and bring hope**.
  *(CT)* — This maps directly onto Brave Ronil's three pillars: **research, family
  support, awareness.**

**What is DIPG?**
> DIPG (Diffuse Intrinsic Pontine Glioma) is a fast-growing tumor that forms in the
> pons, a critical part of the brainstem that controls breathing, heartbeat, and other
> essential functions. Because it grows diffusely — weaving through healthy tissue in a
> vital location — it cannot be surgically removed. It is the #1 deadliest pediatric
> brain tumor, and its prognosis has barely changed in over 60 years.

**What is DMG?**
> DMG (Diffuse Midline Glioma) is a broader diagnosis for tumors that grow in the
> midline of the brain — the thalamus, brainstem, and spinal cord. Most DIPGs that are
> biopsied (70–90%) fall under the DMG category, and many carry the H3 K27M mutation, a
> strong indicator of a difficult prognosis.

**Why it's so devastating**
> DIPG leaves children mentally intact but steals their essential abilities — to walk,
> stand, eat, drink, and even talk — while their awareness remains. There is no cure,
> and treatment options are limited to radiation, which helps most children for a
