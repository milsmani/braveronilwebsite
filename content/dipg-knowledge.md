## 1. What DIPG is

- **DIPG** = **Diffuse Intrinsic Pontine Glioma** — an aggressive brain tumor that
  grows in the **pons**, part of the **brainstem**.
- Under the updated WHO classification (CNS 2021), most DIPG is now formally called
  **DMG — Diffuse Midline Glioma, H3 K27-altered**. "DIPG" describes location/appearance;
  "DMG" reflects the defining molecular change. (The YT Foundation uses "DMG.")
- The brainstem controls the body's most vital automatic functions: **breathing,
  heart rate, blood pressure, swallowing, eye movement, facial movement, balance,
  and the nerves connecting brain to body.** A tumor here is devastating because of
  *where* it grows, not just how fast.
- It is **diffuse and infiltrative** — it weaves through healthy tissue rather than
  forming a removable lump, which is why it **cannot be surgically removed**.

## 2. Who it affects

- Primarily **children**, most often ages **5–10** (peak around 6–9). Can rarely
  occur in teens and adults.
- Roughly **300–400 children per year in the U.S.** are diagnosed (sources cite
  200–400); more worldwide.
- Accounts for **~10–15% of all pediatric brain tumors**.
- It is the **leading cause of brain-tumor-related death in children.**

## 3. Prognosis (why urgency matters)

- **Median survival is about 9–12 months** from diagnosis.
- **Less than 10%** of children survive 2 years.
- **Less than 1%** survive 5 years.
- These survival numbers have **barely changed in decades** — DIPG is one of the few
  cancers where outcomes have not meaningfully improved, largely due to underfunding
  and research barriers.

## 4. Symptoms (often rapid onset — weeks to a few months)

Classic signs cluster in three groups:

- **Cranial nerve problems:** facial weakness/drooping, **double vision (diplopia)**,
  trouble with eye movement, difficulty swallowing or speaking.
- **Long-tract / motor signs:** limb weakness, clumsiness, abnormal reflexes.
- **Cerebellar signs:** loss of balance and coordination (**ataxia**), unsteady walking.
- Others: morning headaches, nausea/vomiting, fatigue.

The **speed** of onset is a hallmark — symptoms can escalate over just weeks.

## 5. Diagnosis

- Diagnosed primarily by **MRI**, which shows a characteristic diffuse pontine tumor —
  often confident enough that historically **biopsy was avoided** due to the risky
  location.
- Today, **biopsy is more common** to obtain tissue for **molecular profiling**
  (e.g., testing for the H3 K27M mutation), which guides trial eligibility and research.

## 6. Treatment

- **Radiation therapy is the standard of care.** It temporarily shrinks the tumor and
  eases symptoms in most children — a **"honeymoon period"** of weeks to months — but
  it is **not a cure**; the tumor almost always regrows.
- **Surgery is not an option** (diffuse growth in an inoperable, vital location).
- **Chemotherapy** has **not** shown meaningful benefit in DIPG.
- **Experimental / trial approaches** (active research areas): re-irradiation,
  **ONC201 / dordaviprone** (for H3 K27M tumors), **CAR-T cell therapy (e.g., GD2)**,
  **convection-enhanced delivery (CED)** to bypass the blood–brain barrier, targeted
  agents, and immunotherapy. Clinical trials are central to progress.

## 7. Why DIPG is so hard to treat (the research barriers)

- **Location:** inoperable — surgery would damage vital brainstem functions.
- **Diffuse infiltration:** no clean tumor margin to remove or target.
- **Blood–brain barrier:** blocks many drugs from reaching the tumor.
- **Molecular biology:** ~**80% carry the H3 K27M mutation** (histone H3.3/H3.1),
  which drives the cancer and is hard to drug.
- **Historic lack of tissue:** because biopsies were long avoided, researchers had
  little material to study — a gap only recently closing.
- **Underfunding:** childhood cancers, and DIPG especially, receive a small fraction
  of cancer research dollars.

## 8. The case for hope (framing for a foundation)

- Molecular understanding (H3 K27M) has transformed DIPG research in the last ~decade.
- Growing tissue banks, better trial designs, and new modalities (CED, CAR-T, ONC201)
  are the first real momentum in a generation.
- **Research funding, family support, and awareness** — the foundation's three
  pillars — directly address the three reasons DIPG has lagged: too little money,
  too little support, too little attention.
